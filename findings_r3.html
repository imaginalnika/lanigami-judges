Findings (descriptive)

  • Instruct judges never picked a Qwen base sample. 0 of 36 votes for each of the four judges (chance 33%). Their votes went to other instruct candidates: Opus 4.6 → GPT-6.1 Sol's text 67%; Opus 5.5 → Sol 50% / its own 50%; Sol → its own 75%; GPT-4o spread over all four instruct texts (28% own).
  • Opus 4.6, Opus 5.5 and Sol mostly agree with each other on the top pick (80%, 100%, 67% of tasks; Sol's text is the most-picked). GPT-4o agrees with each of them on 25%; it is also the least order-consistent (0% same top pick when the order is reversed). Mean order-consistency over all instruct judge × prompt cells: 60%.
  • Base prefills picked a Qwen base sample 18% of the time on average (chance 33%). Letter-slot prefills 18–28%; the k-example reasoning prefills 2–7% (they chose Opus 4.6 and Sol texts about 70% of the time). So here the base prefills lean away from base-written text, less sharply than the instruct judges, and the CoT variants lean the same direction as the instruct judges.
  • Agreement with instruct judges (top pick, chance ≈17%). Base prefill vs instruct judge: 34% on average; instruct vs instruct: 54% (about 82% among the three non-GPT-4o judges). Highest base prefills: reading-group 60%, forum 55%, kex-neutral-cot 46%, lit-review 46%, kex-middle/label 42%; lowest: texture-primed 4%, preface 13%.
  • Neutral worked examples (kex-neutral-cot) gave 46% agreement with instruct judges, 3% Qwen-sample picks, 90% label mass (the three plain/middle/ornate k-example variants gave 28–42%, 2–7%).
  • Letter position still matters. Mean probability on letter A: 36% raw (chance 17%), 22% after calibration. Calibration did not make the reversed-order top pick agree more often (single-order agreement 15% raw, 11% calibrated) and moved agreement with instruct judges from 34% to 32%.
  • Likelihood scoring. Raw mean logprob under the neutral prefix picks the GPT-4o text in 5 of 6 tasks (the most predictable text); Qwen's own samples have the lowest mean logprob (−2.55 vs −1.63 to −1.92) because they were sampled at temperature 1. Good-minus-neutral shifts are all within ±0.03 nats/token. Scoring rules agreed with instruct judges on 6–53% of tasks (pmi-good-vs-neutral 53%).

Caveats

  • Origin cues are still present. 9 of 24 instruct texts contain Markdown (bold, separators) and 11 contain em dashes; 0 of 12 base samples do. The instruct texts have about 6 paragraph breaks against 3. I did not strip formatting from candidates; the cut and clean-up rules below apply equally to both origins.
  • Same 300-token budget for everyone: base samples hit the limit in 12/12 cases and 11 were trimmed back to the last full sentence; 21/24 instruct texts were cut at 300 Qwen-tokens and trimmed the same way. Raw outputs are on each task page.
  • Instruct outputs were echoing the prompt (and sometimes adding a title); everything up to and including the echoed "In the beginning," was removed so every candidate is a continuation. A leading Markdown asterisk pair was also stripped in one case (Opus 5.5, X1). Opus 5.5 returned empty text once at the first token cap (jm-X1) and was regenerated with a larger cap.
  • The k-example prefills' two worked myths (written by the assistant) were re-expressed in Josh's opener template with their candidates re-cut as continuations, so the demonstration matches the task format. Translation worked examples are unchanged. Examples were written by the assistant, not by Josh.
  • Instruct judge settings: temperature 1.0 (Opus, GPT-4o) when writing; Sol left at its default. Judge calls unchanged from earlier rounds.
