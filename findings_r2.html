Descriptive only. No verdict is marked right or wrong. Small numbers: 16 original tasks, plus 12 new myth tasks (6 per origin-controlled set). Round-2 spend is on the bottom of this page.

1 · Likelihood scoring (position bias removed by construction)

  • What worked: Tinker's OpenAI-style completions endpoint silently ignores echo and prompt_logprobs. The Tinker SDK's native compute_logprobs returns logprobs for prompt tokens, so each candidate was scored as prefix + candidate (candidate tokens only, mean per token) under 1 neutral, 4 "good" and 2 "bad" prefixes, for all 28 tasks.
  • Raw likelihood mostly measures where the text came from. The neutral-prefix winner was a Qwen-base candidate in 62% of the 16 original tasks (chance 32%) and in all 6 mixed-origin myths; instruct-written myths average about −2.0 to −2.2 nats/token against about −1.0 for the base samples. In the all-Qwen set, mean logprob falls steeply with sampling temperature (0.6: −0.73, 0.8: −1.04, 1.0: −2.63, 1.2: −5.46), and the raw rule chose the 0.6 sample most often (56%).
  • The quality prefixes barely move the score. Mean shift against the neutral prefix is between −0.026 and +0.014 nats/token for the four "good" prefixes and +0.018 / +0.002 for the two "bad" ones (sd across candidates 0.06–0.11). The "first draft rejected by the editor" prefix scored the candidates slightly higher than the "anthology of the best" prefix. So the PMI (good − neutral) and contrast (good − bad) rules mostly rank noise: they pick a base-origin candidate in 6% (PMI) and 38% (contrast) of the 16 tasks and agree with each instruct judge's top pick on roughly 7–36% of tasks (about chance).
  • Mean logprob correlates with length (Spearman with word count: 0.32 for translations, 0.73 for myths on the raw rule); the PMI and contrast rules do not (−0.3 to +0.05).

2 · Prestige-anchored prefills, and the neutral worked-example variant

  • The four prestige slot prefills are more order-stable than most round-1 slot prefills: order-consistent on 55% (preface), 54% (reading group), 46% (prize citation) and 42% (critic persona) of tasks, against 0–50% for round-1 slot prefills (bare "The better one is" 0%, forum 50%). They put 62–82% of their probability on the letters. The two-critics-then-senior-editor format is 31% order-consistent, writes arguments for both sides, and gave the base-origin myths only 10% of its probability.
  • Agreement with the instruct judges' top picks: reading group 60%, critic persona 54%, kex-neutral 54%, the rest 45% (round-1 slot prefills 14–47%).
  • kex-neutral-cot (notes only describe features; one plain and one rich winner; written by the assistant): ornateness rank of the pick 0.53 and picks the longest candidate 21% of the time (chance 24%), against 13% / 14% / 18% for the plain / middle / ornate example sets. It still gave the instruct-written myths 96% of its probability, like the other note-writing prompts, and was 44% order-consistent.

3 · Calibrated letter judging

  • Content-free priors (all candidates "N/A") confirm large letter biases for some prefills. Bare "The better one is" put 82% on A; calibration brings that to 31% and raises its top-pick agreement with the instruct judges from 14% to 42%. Several round-1 prefills had 40–90% on A in the origin-controlled sets.
  • Order-consistency did not recover. Bare-better stays at 0–6% after calibration. The most order-stable prefills lost consistency (preface 55%→44%, reading group 54%→19%, prize citation 46%→19%, forum 50%→44%). Where the label mass is low, dividing by the prior over-corrects (kex-middle-label's P(A) falls from 36% to 1% and its order-consistency to 0%).

4 · Origin removed (all-Qwen-base set, all-Opus-5.5 set, 6 openers each)

  • Instruct judges lose their consensus. Pairwise agreement among the four instruct judges falls from 89% on the 6 mixed-origin myths to 47% (all-Qwen-base) and 56% (all-Opus-5.5). All four judges agree on a single top pick in 0 of 3 and 1 of 2 tasks where each has a unique pick.
  • Base prefills vs instruct judges: 68% (mixed) → 42% and 53%. That is below instruct-vs-instruct (47%, 56%) and above base-vs-base (37%, 47%). Calibrated prefills: 44% / 42%. The best individual prefills: prize citation (61%, 71%), reading group (45%, 65%), kex-neutral (66%, 45%), preface (46%, 65%). So with origin controlled, base prefills agree with instruct judges at about the rate the instruct judges agree with each other, with n of 6 tasks per set.
  • Order-consistency without origin cues: instruct judge×prompt 57% / 50%, base prefills 28% / 23%, calibrated base 15% / 10%.
  • Length: in the all-Opus-5.5 set (candidates 147–172 words) Opus 5.5 picked the longest candidate in 89% of tasks and Sol in 83% (ornate rank 0.61 for both), Opus 4.6 56%, GPT-4o 22% (chance 25%). Base prefills averaged 37% (23% in the all-Qwen-base set). In the all-Qwen-base set the instruct judges did not favor length (Opus 4.6 6%, Opus 5.5 33%, Sol 28%, GPT-4o 28%).
  • Temperature in the all-Qwen-base set: instruct judges gave 49% of their preference to the temperature-1.0 sample, 26% / 25% to 0.6 / 0.8 and none to 1.2; base prefills gave 35% to 1.0 and 17% to 1.2.

5 · Folding

  • Qwen base (two zero-shot prefills, 4 samples each) writes a combined text on all 28 tasks, alongside one Opus 5.5 and one GPT-6.1 Sol fold. Translation folds are 20–22 words on average (candidates average 20). Myth folds are about 240 words from Qwen base, 177 from Opus 5.5 and 404 from Sol (candidates average 165). Sol's myth folds are about 2.4× the mean candidate length.
  • By word overlap, myth folds from Qwen base are nearest to a Qwen-base candidate about 42–49% of the time and to an Opus 5.5 candidate about 42%; some are close copies of one candidate. Outputs are on the Per task page; nothing is judged.

Caveats

  • The worked examples in every k-example prefill (including the neutral variant) and the persona texts were written by the assistant, an instruct model.
  • The all-Opus-5.5 set was generated by one of the judges, so any self-preference applies to every candidate there.
  • The all-Qwen-base set used the same mechanical filter as round 1 (no CJK or markdown bold, at least 90 words). It lets through some wiki-style headings and, at temperature 1.2, content that drifts far from the opener (see mythB-2 C4). Judges avoiding the 1.2 samples is therefore not surprising.
  • Origin-controlled agreement rests on 6 tasks per set; ties are skipped.
  • Calibration was applied to the slot prefills only, not to the sampled note-writing prefills.
