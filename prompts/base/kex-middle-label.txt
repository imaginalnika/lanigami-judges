--- Item 1 ---
Opening: In the world of Brindle Quay, strange tales are told of the first bell.

A. Before the first bell, the Quay was an unvoiced hush, a vast and tremulous silence woven of fog and longing; then the Great Smith, forging in the cathedral of the sea, wrung from molten moonlight a resplendent, thundering heart of bronze, whose song unmade the dark.
B. It was a dull piece of tin that fell off a cart. A child hit it with a spoon to see what would happen, and the sound went down the street faster than she could. The whole quay turned round to look. That is why we ring it now, to give everyone the same surprise.
C. A smith made the first bell from the melted anchors of ships that never came home. When he struck it, the harbour went quiet, and then every gull on the Quay answered at once. He said he hadn't meant it to sing; he'd only wanted it to stop the drowning.

Keep: C

--- Item 2 ---
Opening: In the world of the Long Larder, strange tales are told of the first loaf.

A. The first loaf was an accident of damp flour and a warm shelf. Nobody knew it was bread; they thought it was a small animal that had died of happiness. The baker's daughter was the only one brave enough to taste it.
B. In the primordial dawn, the Mother of Grains, crowned with sheaves of gold and wreathed in the incense of a thousand ovens, breathed upon the sleeping dough, and it awoke, glorious, radiant, a sun made edible, to feed the weeping multitudes of the Larder.
C. A woman left her porridge by the fire and forgot it. It rose and cracked and smelled like a promise. Her husband ate the first piece out of spite and the second out of shame.

Keep: A
